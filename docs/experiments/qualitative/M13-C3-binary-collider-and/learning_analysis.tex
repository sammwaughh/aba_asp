\section{Contract}

Fixture \texttt{m13\_bucket3\_binary\_collider\_and} has DAG
\(A\to C\leftarrow B\), with
\(A\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(4/5)\) and \(B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(7/10)\)
independent, and with deterministic child \(C=A\land B\).
The frozen sample has size \(n{=}30\) and seed~42.
Encoding is exact-value BK with binary 1-vs-0 examples.
Both arms use brave learning and \texttt{check\_ic}.
The AAMAS arm is \texttt{aamas2025} (greedy / mgr / bk).
The ECAI arm is \texttt{ecai2024} (nd / any / all).
The evaluator-only reference for \(c\) is
\texttt{c(A) :- a\_val\_1(A), b\_val\_1(A).}.
Roots are marked \texttt{no\_observed\_parent\_deterministic\_rule}.

Collection roots lie under
\path{causal/outputs/aba_learning/targetwise/m13_bucket3_binary_collider_and/{aamas2025,ecai2024}/n30_seed42/}.
Arm \texttt{summary.md} files are the authoritative outcome tables.

\section{Geometry for learning}

Sample counts are \((1,1,1):17\), \((1,0,0):7\), \((0,1,0):4\), and
\((0,0,0):2\) (rows~26 and~30 only).
The implied \(E^\pm\) sizes are \(a\) 24/6, \(b\) 21/9, and \(c\) 17/13.

Under \(C=A\land B\), every positive example for \(c\) lies in the single
predictor family \((A,B)=(1,1)\).
Positive examples for root \(a\) split into \((B,C)=(1,1)\) and \((B,C)=(0,0)\)
(and symmetrically for \(b\)).
The cell \((\textit{other root},C)=(0,0)\) is label-ambiguous.
For target \(a\), row~9 with values \((1,0,0)\) is \(E^+\), while row~26 with
values \((0,0,0)\) is \(E^-\), yet both share the BK literals
\texttt{b\_val\_0} and \texttt{c\_val\_0}.
Roots have no deterministic causal rule over the other observed variables.
A syntactic match between a delta and the evaluator reference for \(c\) is not
automatic causal success.

\section{AAMAS narrative}

{\raggedright
The AAMAS arm uses \texttt{configs/aamas2025\_config.pl}.
The active options for these cells are \texttt{learning\_mode(brave)},
\texttt{folding\_mode(greedy)}, \texttt{folding\_selection(mgr)},
\texttt{folding\_space(bk)}, \texttt{asm\_intro(relto)}, and \texttt{check\_ic}.
Under greedy folding with BK space, each positive example is replaced by a
\textbf{maximal co-occurrence body} assembled from background predicates that
hold of that row.
The mgr selection step then keeps a most-general representative among duplicate
bodies.
Brave entailment is the acceptance test for each candidate extension.
Authoritative traces are the per-cell \texttt{prolog.stdout} files under
\path{.../aamas2025/n30_seed42/cells/target-{a,b,c}/output/}, together
with the arm \texttt{summary.md}.
\par}

\subsection{Target \texorpdfstring{\(c\)}{c} --- solved with an assumption-free conjunction}

For target \(c\), every positive example lies in the single joint cell
\((A,B)=(1,1)\).
There are seventeen such rows, and each therefore admits the same maximal BK
body \(\{\mathtt{a\_val\_1},\mathtt{b\_val\_1}\}\).
After mgr deduplication, the learner retains one live candidate:
\begin{verbatim}
c(A) :- a_val_1(A), b_val_1(A).
\end{verbatim}
Brave entailment of \(\langle E^+,E^-\rangle\) succeeds immediately.
No negative example shares that body, so no assumption is introduced.
The folding queue is empty, and the run terminates as \texttt{solved} with zero
assumptions and zero contraries.
The post-run artefact audit on \texttt{bk.sol\_chk.asp} reports SAT.

The emitted delta string is identical to the evaluator-only positive-state
reference for \(c\) in \texttt{mechanism\_reference.json}.
That match is recorded here as a \textbf{syntactic coincidence}.
It is not by itself a causal-recovery verdict.
The same configuration and sample can fail on the root targets, as the next
subsection shows.

Authoritative artefacts:
\path{.../aamas2025/n30_seed42/cells/target-c/output/{delta.aba,prolog.stdout}}
and the arm \texttt{summary.md}.

\subsection{Targets \texorpdfstring{\(a\)}{a}/\texorpdfstring{\(b\)}{b} --- \texttt{completed\_no\_solution} (symmetric)}

The story for root \(a\) is verified in
\path{.../aamas2025/n30_seed42/cells/target-a/output/prolog.stdout}.
Target \(b\) is the same procedure with the roles of \(A\) and \(B\) swapped.

Positive examples for \(a\) fall into two BK co-occurrence families.
The first is \((B,C)=(1,1)\), which arises on every row where \(A=1\) and the
AND child fires.
The second is \((B,C)=(0,0)\), which arises on rows such as row~9 where \(A=1\)
yet \(B=C=0\).
Greedy folding therefore proposes two distinct bodies.

The both-1 rule
\begin{verbatim}
a(A) :- b_val_1(A), c_val_1(A).
\end{verbatim}
is accepted under brave entailment.
It covers the \((1,1)\) positives without touching any negative example.

The both-0 rule
\begin{verbatim}
a(A) :- b_val_0(A), c_val_0(A).
\end{verbatim}
is then proposed from an early both-0 positive (the trace folds row~8).
That body is \textbf{not} a function of the root.
It also holds of the negative examples in rows~\textbf{26} and~\textbf{30},
which are the only sample atoms \((0,0,0)\).
Brave entailment therefore fails.
The learner introduces an assumption \(\alpha_1\) relative to the offending
body, yielding
\begin{verbatim}
a(A) :- alpha_1(A), b_val_0(A), c_val_0(A).
\end{verbatim}
Contrary rote learning then cites exactly rows~26 and~30 as grounds for
\(\mathtt{c\_alpha\_1}\).
Greedy folding of those contrary examples reconstructs the \textbf{same}
both-0 body for the contrary,
\begin{verbatim}
c_alpha_1(A) :- b_val_0(A), c_val_0(A).
\end{verbatim}
Under \texttt{asm\_intro(relto)}, a further assumption cannot be introduced for
that body because \(\alpha_1\) is already present.
The engine reports KO and ends with \texttt{* No solution found!}.
The cell outcome is \texttt{completed\_no\_solution}.
No \texttt{delta.aba} and no \texttt{bk.sol.*} artefacts are emitted.

This is a \textbf{search and repair dead-end} on this example geometry under
greedy, mgr, and relto.
It is not a runner failure.
The folding procedure is the same as for target \(c\).
What differs is the split of \(E^+\) across two predictor cells and the
presence of opposite labels inside the both-0 cell.

\subsection{Target \texorpdfstring{\(c\)}{c} --- solved by under-folding and a contrary}

The path is verified in
\path{.../ecai2024/n30_seed42/cells/target-c/output/prolog.stdout}
and in the corresponding \texttt{delta.aba}.

Learning begins from rote positive rules indexed by sample id.
The first selected positive is row~1.
Nd folding replaces that id by \(\mathtt{a\_val\_1}\), which is the first BK
predictor under the declared order \(a\), then \(b\).
The resulting unary rule is
\begin{verbatim}
c(A) :- a_val_1(A).
\end{verbatim}
That body is too strong for the AND child.
Every row with \(A=1\) and \(B=0\) is an \(E^-\) example for \(c\), yet
satisfies \(\mathtt{a\_val\_1}\).
Brave entailment fails, and the learner introduces \(\alpha_1\), giving
\begin{verbatim}
c(A) :- alpha_1(A), a_val_1(A).
\end{verbatim}
Contrary rote learning then records \(\mathtt{c\_alpha\_1}\) on the seven
\((1,0,0)\) negatives that remain uncovered attacks, namely
rows~\textbf{8, 9, 13, 14, 18, 24,} and~\textbf{25}.

The first contrary fold attempt replaces row~8 by \(\mathtt{a\_val\_1}\).
That candidate fails, and relto cannot introduce a second assumption for that
body.
A subsequent fold replaces the same contrary example by \(\mathtt{b\_val\_0}\).
Brave entailment then succeeds.
All remaining contrary rotes are subsumed, and the queue empties.

The final delta is
\begin{verbatim}
c(A) :- alpha_1(A), a_val_1(A).
c_alpha_1(A) :- b_val_0(A).
assumption(alpha_1(A)).
contrary(alpha_1(A),c_alpha_1(A)) :- assumption(alpha_1(A)).
\end{verbatim}
On this sample, the framework behaves extensionally like ``derive \(c\) when
\(a=1\), unless the assumption is attacked when \(b=0\)''.
Under brave checking of the observed \(E^\pm\), that presentation is aligned
with \(C=A\land B\).
The ABA \textbf{shape} is nevertheless different from the AAMAS conjunction.
In particular, the delta string does \textbf{not} match the evaluator reference
\texttt{c(A) :- a\_val\_1(A), b\_val\_1(A).}.
The run reports \texttt{solved} with artefact-audit SAT.

\subsection{Targets \texorpdfstring{\(a\)}{a}/\texorpdfstring{\(b\)}{b} --- solved by a brave choice gadget}

The path for root \(a\) is verified in
\path{.../ecai2024/n30_seed42/cells/target-a/output/{prolog.stdout,delta.aba}}.
Target \(b\) is the mirror with \(a\leftrightarrow b\).
The arm summary records eleven delta rules, three assumptions, and three
contraries for each root, with artefact-audit SAT.

Nd folding again takes one step at a time.
From an early \((B,C)=(1,1)\) positive (row~1), the first fold yields the unary
body \(\mathtt{b\_val\_1}\).
That rule over-generalises onto \(E^-\) rows where \(B=1\) and \(C=0\)
(the trace cites rows~3, 5, 15, and~19 for the first contrary).
Assumption \(\alpha_1\) is introduced, and the contrary later folds
successfully to \(\mathtt{c\_val\_0}\).
The resulting block separates the safe both-1 positives from the \(B=1,C=0\)
negatives:
\begin{verbatim}
a(A) :- alpha_1(A), b_val_1(A).
c_alpha_1(A) :- c_val_0(A).
\end{verbatim}

A second ordinary target rule is then required for the remaining both-0
positives.
Folding an early both-0 positive (row~8) yields \(\mathtt{b\_val\_0}\).
Brave entailment fails because the same BK literals hold of the \(E^-\) atoms
in rows~\textbf{26} and~\textbf{30}.
Assumption \(\alpha_2\) is introduced on that body.
Contrary rote cites those two negatives.
Folding the contrary first attempts \(\mathtt{b\_val\_0}\) (KO under relto
against \(\alpha_2\)), then \(\mathtt{c\_val\_0}\).
Even the unary \(\mathtt{c\_val\_0}\) contrary is still too strong for brave
entailment of the joint example set, so a further assumption \(\alpha_3\)
appears on the contrary.
Rote learning of \(\mathtt{c\_alpha\_3}\) then cites the both-0
\textbf{positives} (rows~8, 9, 13, 14, 18, 24, and~25).
That contrary folds by re-using \(\alpha_2\) together with
\(\mathtt{b\_val\_0}\).
The resulting nest is
\begin{verbatim}
a(A) :- alpha_2(A), b_val_0(A).
c_alpha_2(A) :- alpha_3(A), c_val_0(A).
c_alpha_3(A) :- alpha_2(A), b_val_0(A).
\end{verbatim}
together with the corresponding \texttt{assumption} and \texttt{contrary}
declarations.

The important geometric point is the ambiguous cell \((B,C)=(0,0)\).
Row~\textbf{9} is an \(E^+\) example for \(a\) with joint values \((1,0,0)\).
Row~\textbf{26} is an \(E^-\) example with joint values \((0,0,0)\).
Both rows share the BK literals \(\mathtt{b\_val\_0}\) and
\(\mathtt{c\_val\_0}\).
No deterministic rule over \((B,C)\) can assign both labels.
The \(\alpha_2\)/\(\alpha_3\) nest is a \textbf{per-row assumption choice
gadget}.
Within one witnessing stable model, the ground assumption atoms for different
row identifiers can take different statuses, permitting \(a\) to hold at row~9
while being rejected at row~26.
Other stable models may make different choices.
Brave entailment requires the existence of at least one stable model that meets
the \textbf{joint} \(E^\pm\) constraints; it does not use a separate witness for
each example and does \textbf{not} require a unique determination of \(a\) from
\((b,c)\).

The runner outcome \texttt{solved} therefore means that a serialized ABA
framework passed brave entailment and that the artefact audit reported SAT.
That outcome is \textbf{misleading} if it is read as recovery of a functional
root mechanism.
The evaluator reference for roots remains
\texttt{no\_observed\_parent\_deterministic\_rule}.

rule, and fails entirely.
ECAI emits a non-functional brave covering of the same ambiguous cell.
Neither recovers a root mechanism.
All six cells use the same frozen table.
The differences are strategy, entailment semantics, and target geometry, not
resampling.
The \texttt{sol\_chk} SAT result is an artefact-integrity audit, not a separate
coverage metric.
