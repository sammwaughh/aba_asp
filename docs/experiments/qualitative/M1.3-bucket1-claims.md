## Preamble: notions of success

Throughout the Bucket 1 claims, four notions of success are kept distinct. They are
not interchangeable, and no claim treats runner completion as mechanism recovery.

**Procedural `solved`.** The grid runner labels a cell `solved` when ABA Learning
terminates and writes a solution artefact (`bk.sol.*`). That label records
completion of the learning procedure. It does not entail full ASP coverage of
\(\langle E^+,E^-\rangle\), exact recovery of \(\mathcal{H}_t^\star\), or semantic
success under the locked M1.2 description.

**ASP coverage.** After learning, positives covered and negatives rejected are
checked under brave entailment on `bk.sol.asp`. Full coverage means the learned
framework separates the example sets. Incomplete coverage (in particular, failure
to reject negatives) may coexist with runner `solved`, as in ECAI U7 on \(x_3\).

**Exact match to \(\mathcal{H}_t^\star\).** Exact syntactic agreement with the locked
reference rule set is recorded when it occurs. It is not the primary acceptance
criterion for M1.3. Most cells that are scientifically interesting under the
semantic success description are not exact matches.

**Semantic success.** Acceptance remains inspection-first against the locked M1.2
semantic success description for the cell. That description concerns whether the
learned framework encodes the intended mechanism acceptably in the `val` language
(including, where relevant, assumption-mediated presentations). Descendant citation
falls outside semantic success. Exact match to \(\mathcal{H}_t^\star\) is neither
necessary nor sufficient for semantic success.

Claims below use these four labels exactly as stated. Where a cell is
runner-`solved` but fails ASP coverage, exact match, or semantic success, that
failure is named explicitly.

## Claim 1 — What AAMAS learned across M12x

**Claim.** Across all nine AAMAS M12x cells, under `folding_mode(greedy)`,
`folding_selection(mgr)`, and `folding_space(bk)`, each positive row \(i\) is
specialised to a target rule whose body cites the exact-value predicate of every
non-target variable true on that row. Consequently
\(\mathrm{Pa}(t)\subseteq\operatorname{Vars}(B_r)\) for every learned target
rule \(r\), and no assumptions are introduced. Exact recovery of
\(\mathcal{H}_t^\star\) occurs only on U2, where every available predictor is a
parent and the locked reference already has the same positive-cell conjunction
form.

**Argument.** Under greedy folding the engine walks each foldable body literal
and accumulates every successful replacement by an eligible background head.
With `folding_space(bk)`, those heads are drawn only from the original
exact-value background. There is no later literal-pruning or rule-compression
stage. On the locked M12x tables, the resulting row-wise conjunctions already
separate \(\langle E^+,E^-\rangle\), so `gen2` accepts them without Assumption
Introduction.

The Stage-3 inspections of cells 10–18 confirm the consequences. Parent
variables appear in every AAMAS target body. In seven of the nine cells the
bodies also retain a non-parent (isolated variable, sibling, ancestor, or
descendant). On U3 the bodies cite only parents, yet the learned theory is an
eight-clause positive-cell DNF rather than the compact unary OR of
\(\mathcal{H}_{x_2}^\star\). Exact match occurs solely on U2 (cell 11), where
the four both-parent conjunctions coincide with \(\mathcal{H}_{x_2}^\star\).
That coincidence is representational: greedy’s natural output already matches
the chosen reference form, and there are no distractors to retain. It is not
evidence that AAMAS identified the parents as causally privileged.

## Claim 2 — What ECAI learned across M12x

**Claim.** Across all nine ECAI M12x cells, under `folding_mode(nd)` with the
token budget beginning at 1, learning commits early to one-literal folds on
\(x_0\). When such a fold already separates \(\langle E^+,E^-\rangle\), the
engine keeps the corresponding unary target rule without assumptions. When it
overgeneralises, Assumption Introduction under `asm_intro(relto)` patches that
same \(x_0\)-keyed body with an assumption. Contrary learning may then
introduce further conditions, including conditions on other variables, but it
does not replace the ordinary target body by a different backbone. As a result,
every ECAI solution has ordinary target rules keyed by \(x_0\). No cell
produces an ordinary multi-parent conjunction in the target rules.

That shared pattern separates the nine cells by the fixture fact
\(x_0\in\mathrm{Pa}(t)\). Where \(x_0\) is a direct parent of the target—on
U2, U3, both U4 targets, and U6 with target \(x_2\)—the \(x_0\)-keyed
solutions are mechanism-aligned under the locked semantic success descriptions.
Two of those cells are exact matches to \(\mathcal{H}_t^\star\). Where \(x_0\)
is not a direct parent—on U1, U5, U6 with target \(x_3\), and U7—the same
pattern keys the target rules by a non-parent. Those cells are intensionally
misaligned, even when a preferred parent later appears in a contrary, and even
when ASP coverage is complete.

**Argument.** The ECAI configuration uses non-deterministic folding with an
initial token budget of 1. At that budget a successful fold replaces the sample
identifier by a single background literal. On the locked M12x background
knowledge, the Stage-3 traces show that this first success is an exact-value
literal of \(x_0\). Learning therefore begins from unary \(x_0\)-keyed
candidate target rules.

ABA Learning then treats overgeneralisation by Assumption Introduction relative
to the failing body. The body is not discarded. An assumption \(\alpha\) is
added, giving a defeasible rule of the form
\(t(A)\leftarrow x_0\text{_val\_}v(A),\alpha(A)\). Contrary learning may
afterwards restrict when \(\alpha\) holds, and those contrary conditions may
mention other variables. In the ABA framework those contraries undercut the
default. They do not substitute a new ordinary target backbone for the \(x_0\)
condition already chosen.

Two consequences follow for every ECAI cell. First, the ordinary target rules
remain keyed by \(x_0\). Second, because repair proceeds around that one-literal
backbone, the learner never emits an ordinary conjunction of several parent
literals in the target rules. The Stage-3 inspections of cells 1–9 confirm both
consequences.

The nine cells then divide on the role of \(x_0\) in the locked graph. On U4,
\(\mathrm{Pa}(t)=\{x_0\}\) for both targets. The unary \(x_0\) folds already
separate the examples, so the solutions are exact matches to
\(\mathcal{H}_t^\star\) without assumptions. On U2, U3, and U6 \(x_2\), one
likewise has \(x_0\in\mathrm{Pa}(t)\). The early folds overgeneralise within
their \(x_0\) slices, and assumptions with contraries complete the encoding.
In those cells a second parent may appear in a contrary as part of a coherent
mechanism-aligned framework. The solutions succeed because the default backbone
is already a direct parent.

On U1, \(x_0\) is isolated. On U5, U6 \(x_3\), and U7, \(x_0\) is a non-parent
ancestor. The same procedure therefore builds a non-parent backbone. A preferred
parent may still appear among the contrary conditions. That does not yield the
intended parent-based target intension once the ordinary rules are keyed by a
non-parent. On U6 \(x_3\) and U7 the second parent \(x_2\) is absent from the
learned framework. U7 also fails to reject negatives under ASP coverage. The
unsuccessful cells are the same ECAI pattern applied where
\(x_0\notin\mathrm{Pa}(t)\).

What ECAI does on M12x is therefore uniform: it learns \(x_0\)-keyed target
rules and uses assumptions and contraries only as repair around that backbone.
What separates the cells is whether that backbone variable is a direct parent
of \(t\).

## Claim 3 — Strong intensional ECAI solutions on U2, U3, and U6 \(x_2\)

**Claim.** On ECAI cells U2, U3, and U6 with target \(x_2\), the learned
frameworks are theoretically defensible intensional solutions to the locked
learning problems, not merely approximate coverage repairs. In each case the
ordinary target rules, assumptions, and contrary-derived undercutting conditions
jointly express the intended Boolean mechanism over the direct parents. The
solutions achieve full ASP coverage. They cite neither sample identifiers nor
non-parent predictors. On U6 \(x_2\) they also leave the descendant unused.
None of the three is an exact match to \(\mathcal{H}_t^\star\). The mismatch is
therefore a difference of intensional presentation within ABA, not a failure to
recover the target concept.

**Argument.** ABA Learning returns a complete assumption-based framework.
Assumption Introduction and contrary learning are licensed transformations in
that setting. Their purpose is to encode defeasible rules through undercutting.
A solution that uses assumptions is therefore not, by that fact alone, an
inferior substitute for an assumption-free rule set. What matters is whether the
resulting framework expresses the intended mechanism.

Claim 2 already places these three cells among the ECAI successes with
\(x_0\in\mathrm{Pa}(t)\). The present claim concerns the quality of those
particular assumption-mediated outputs. The Stage-3 inspections support a
stronger reading than coverage alone.

On U2 the locked reference enumerates both-parents-nonzero as four ordinary
parent conjunctions. The learned framework instead keeps unary nonzero
conditions on \(x_0\) in the target rules and expresses nonzero \(x_1\) through
undefeated assumptions whose contraries fire when \(x_1=0\). On an
\(x_0\in\{1,2\}\) row, \(x_2\) holds if and only if the assumption is undefeated
if and only if \(x_1\neq 0\). That is exactly the nonzero-positive semantics of
\(\min\).

On U6 with target \(x_2\) the mechanism and the recovered intension are the
same. The additional background predicates for the descendant \(x_3\) are never
cited. The framework again characterises both-parents-nonzero by an \(x_0\)-keyed
default together with contrary conditions on \(x_1\).

On U3 the locked reference is a four-clause expansion of
at-least-one-parent-nonzero. The learned framework is an \(x_0\)-first case
analysis. The complete nonzero \(x_0\) slices become ordinary unary rules. Only
the incomplete \(x_0=0\) slice needs an assumption, which encodes \(x_1\neq 0\)
through its contrary. Extensionally this is
\((x_0=0\land x_1\neq 0)\lor(x_0=1)\lor(x_0=2)\), which is exactly \(x_0\neq 0\)
or \(x_1\neq 0\), the nonzero-positive semantics of \(\max\).

In all three cells the examples are fully separated under brave ASP coverage.
The frameworks are variable-based rather than rote sample listings. They remain
within the parent set of \(x_2\). Their departure from \(\mathcal{H}_t^\star\) is
a choice of ABA presentation of the same Boolean concept. It should not be read
as semantic failure.

## Claim 4 — Shared incompleteness on U6 and U7 \(x_3\), exposed only on U7

**Claim.** ECAI cells U6 and U7 with target \(x_3\) share the same parent set
\(\mathrm{Pa}(x_3)=\{x_1,x_2\}\) and, on their respective observed tables, the
same nonzero labelling rule \(x_3\neq 0\) if and only if \(x_1\neq x_2\). Under
ECAI they also share the same structurally incomplete learned pattern. Ordinary
target rules are keyed by the non-parent ancestor \(x_0\). The parent \(x_1\)
appears only in contrary conditions. The parent \(x_2\) is absent from the
learned framework. On U6 that incomplete framework fully separates
\(\langle E^+,E^-\rangle\). On U7 it covers all positives but rejects none of
the three negatives. The coverage divergence is explained by support. On U6 the
pair \((x_0,x_1)\) already determines the \(x_3\) labelling. On U7 it does not.

**Argument.** Both cells fall under Claim 2’s unsuccessful branch:
\(x_0\notin\mathrm{Pa}(x_3)\), so ECAI’s \(x_0\)-first folding builds a
non-parent backbone. The Stage-3 inspections show the same further
incompleteness in both. After Assumption Introduction, contrary repair reaches
conditions on \(x_1\). It never introduces an \(x_2\) literal. Preferred parent
\(x_2\) remains unused on the accepted path. Relative to the locked two-parent
references \(\mathcal{H}_{x_3}^\star\), both solutions are therefore
structurally incomplete in the same way.

The mechanisms make the comparison controlled. On U6 one has
\(x_3:=x_1-x_2\) with \(x_2:=\min(x_0,x_1)\). On U7 one has
\(x_3:=|x_1-x_2|\). In both cases the observed nonzero rule is \(x_3\neq 0\) if
and only if \(x_1\neq x_2\), and the intended parents are \(\{x_1,x_2\}\).

On U6 the table is the factorial over \((x_0,x_1)\). Because
\(x_2=\min(x_0,x_1)\), one has \(x_3=0\) whenever \(x_1\leq x_0\) and \(x_3>0\)
whenever \(x_1>x_0\). Under nonzero-positive labelling, \(x_3\) is therefore
positive exactly when \(x_1>x_0\), and negative otherwise. The pair
\((x_0,x_1)\) has deterministic case-based power over the target labelling. An
\(x_0\)-keyed default repaired by contrary conditions on \(x_1\) can express
that comparison without citing \(x_2\). The learned U6 theory does so, and ASP
coverage is complete. Full separation therefore does not show that the omitted
parent was unnecessary to the intended mechanism. It shows that \(\{x_0,x_1\}\)
already determined the labels on this support.

On U7 that determinacy fails. Each positive has a twin negative that shares
\((x_0,x_1)\) and differs only in \(x_2\). The pair \((x_0,x_1)\) does not
determine positivity for \(x_3\). Any framework that ignores \(x_2\) must treat
some twin pair alike. The same incomplete pattern therefore covers all positives
and rejects none of the three negatives, despite runner `solved`.

U6 and U7 thus exhibit the same incomplete ECAI intension. U6’s coverage is an
artefact of \((x_0,x_1)\) determining the labels via \(x_1>x_0\). U7’s covering
failure is that incompleteness made visible once \((x_0,x_1)\) no longer
determines the labels.
