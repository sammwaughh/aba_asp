\begin{abstract}
This document records the design, implementation, commands, artefacts, and bounded conclusions for experiment~M11 (m1.1). It is a standalone audit log for supervisor review, not a manuscript chapter. The experiment tests whether target-wise ABA Learning on complete noiseless \emph{target-mechanism} tables---one isolated predictor plus a deterministic parent$\rightarrow$\texttt{x2} copy---recovers rules that track the \emph{direct-parent role} under variable-identity ($\pi$) and predictor-order ($\sigma$) transformations. This is \textbf{not} chain SCM sampling or chain discovery; chain-style edge labels in the fixtures annotate parent vs non-parent roles for $\pi$ only. Under fixed non-deterministic folding, binary encoding passes all checks; categorical-3 encoding fails $\sigma$-invariance on both tested edges. Greedy folding as a remedy is deferred to Milestone~1 Part~2 (M1.2).
\end{abstract}

\section{Position in Milestone~1}

Milestone~1 (see \texttt{docs/research/milestone\_plans/milestone1-plan.md}) investigates whether ABA Learning techniques support causal-discovery-adjacent parent-set recovery on controlled fixtures.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Part~1 (m1.1, this document):} metamorphic validity control on the QL2 (QI-002) parent-recovery proxy --- parent identity crossed with learner-visible predictor order.
  \item \textbf{Part~2 (m1.2, planned):} rerun the same eight-cell grid under \texttt{folding\_mode: greedy} and compare to this nd baseline.
\end{itemize}

Motivation: QI-002's categorical chain returned a rule citing \texttt{x0} where the expected direct parent was \texttt{x1}. m1.1 separates direct-parent-role tracking from fixed variable identity and from fixed representation position.

\section{Research question and design}

\subsection{Question}

Under complete, noiseless QL2-style target-mechanism tables, when the direct-parent and non-parent roles are exchanged between \texttt{x0} and \texttt{x1}, and the learner-visible predictor order is independently reversed, does the learnt \texttt{x2} rule follow the variable occupying the direct-parent role?

\subsection{Factorial structure}

Eight deterministic cells: two encodings (\{binary, cat3\}) $\times$ four cells \{A, B, C, D\}, derived from one canonical cell~A per encoding via:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\sigma$: reverse learner-visible predictor order (values, examples, roles unchanged);
  \item $\pi$: exchange \texttt{x0}$\leftrightarrow$\texttt{x1} (names, values, edges relabelled; \texttt{x2} fixed).
\end{itemize}

Tabular DGP (all cells): exhaustive factorial over \((x0,x1)\); \texttt{x2 := parent} deterministically; the non-parent is marginally independent of \texttt{x2}. At the data level each cell is one \emph{isolated} predictor plus a \emph{connected} parent--target pair, not a faithful chain sample.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Cells & Parent & Isolated & Data-level graph \\
\midrule
A, B & \texttt{x1} & \texttt{x0} & \texttt{x0} ; \texttt{x1}$\rightarrow$\texttt{x2} \\
C, D & \texttt{x0} & \texttt{x1} & \texttt{x1} ; \texttt{x0}$\rightarrow$\texttt{x2} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Role labels (for $\pi$ only; not edges in the table): A/B use parent \texttt{x1}; C/D use parent \texttt{x0}. Chain-style annotations in the fixture code label parent vs non-parent; they do not generate ancestor--parent association in the table.

Minimal complete factorial: binary 4 rows; cat3 9 rows; one row per \((x0,x1)\) assignment; no noise; seed~0.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}

\midrule
A, B & \texttt{x2(A) <- x1(A)} & \texttt{x2(A) <- x1\_val\_2(A)} \\
C, D & \texttt{x2(A) <- x0(A)} & \texttt{x2(A) <- x0\_val\_2(A)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These are the unique intended one-literal observable-feature separators in each cell, not uniqueness over the full ABA hypothesis space.

\subsection{Fixed learner settings}

Target \texttt{x2} only; \texttt{folding\_mode: nd}; \texttt{folding\_steps: 15}; \texttt{prolog\_timeout\_s: 120}. Inherited QI-002-style strategy. \textbf{Not} Russo-style Causal ABA (no \texttt{arr}/\texttt{noe}/\texttt{indep}, d-separation, or stable-extension-as-DAG machinery).

\section{Results statement (facts)}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item All eight fixtures pass Stage-0; requested predictor order equals table order equals BK group order; $\sigma$ and $\pi$ orbit relations hold on fixtures.
  \item All eight learning cells \texttt{solved} under nd folding.
  \item Binary: 4/4 exact expected singleton; all four $\sigma$ and $\pi$ edges hold; \texttt{cov\_py} and \texttt{cov\_pl} agree.
  \item Cat3: 2/4 exact (B, C); A and D return three-rule assumption/contrary structures citing the non-parent; $\sigma$ fails on both tested pairs; $\pi$ holds on B$\leftrightarrow$C only.
  \item Triggered inspection documents that nd folding's first literal tried follows BK predicate-block order, which determines the entailment branch on cat3 A vs B.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Interpretation}

\subsection{Binary}

Positive control: under these fixtures and fixed nd configuration, returned rules track the direct-parent role across $\pi$ swaps and are invariant to tested predictor order ($\sigma$). This supports parent-\emph{role} tracking in the binary encoding under the stated controls. It is \textbf{not} causal discovery and \textbf{not} full Causal ABA.

\subsection{Categorical-3}

$\sigma$-invariance \textbf{fails}: reversing predictor order while fixing parent identity changes exact singleton vs assumption-based non-parent rule. The split is encoding-dependent (binary passes; cat3 does not). Stage~3 points to BK serialisation order $\rightarrow$ first nd-fold literal $\rightarrow$ entailment vs assumption introduction as the observable mechanism. Same structural class as QI-002 \texttt{chain\_cat3}.

\subsection{Overall bounded answer}

m1.1 answers its central question \textbf{conditionally}: direct-parent tracking with representation-order invariance holds for \textbf{binary} but not for \textbf{cat3} under fixed non-deterministic folding.

\subsection{Explicit non-claims}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Not chain SCM sampling, chain discovery, or whole-graph recovery.
  \item Not Russo-style Causal ABA.
  \item Not a claim that greedy folding fixes cat3 $\sigma$ failure (M1.2 hypothesis only).
  \item Not a test of correlated-ancestor / faithful chain SCM behaviour (QI-003/QI-004 territory).
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Single seed; no noise; chain motif only; target-wise learning on \texttt{x2} only.
  \item Assumption-based solutions are \texttt{solved} but discrepant vs the pre-specified singleton criterion.
  \item Coverage metrics disagree on cat3 A/D assumption rules; classifications rely on sol files and traces.
  \item Whether a different nd fold-literal order would recover cat3 A/D without assumptions was not tested.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why this justifies M1.2 now}

m1.1 isolates an encoding-dependent representation-order sensitivity under nd on cat3: non-parent-first BK order leads nd to try the isolated predictor’s literal first, miss entailment, and introduce assumptions. Milestone~1 Part~2 will rerun the same grid (or cat3 $\sigma$-cells) under \texttt{folding\_mode: greedy}.

Prior qualitative evidence (QI-002 greedy rerun) shows greedy can change cat3 behaviour on other motifs (e.g.\ binary collider no-solution $\rightarrow$ solved; cat3 fork regression). That precedent motivates but does \textbf{not} establish greedy as a fix for m1.1 cat3 $\sigma$ failure --- claim M11-C-004 remains \textbf{tentative} until M1.2 runs.
